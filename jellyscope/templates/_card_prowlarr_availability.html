{# The Prowlarr card "Indexer availability": how long every indexer was available in the period, and its days. From the stored samples of the indexers' status (cards_prowlarr.py, `d.rows`; stats.prowlarr_availability): the share of the measured time Prowlarr did not skip the indexer, how many outages, the longest one, the last failure (UTC, written in the app's zone) and the share of failed queries from the history. An indexer whose outage still runs has "unavailable" by its name - not by the longest outage, which may be an older one. Ready texts, "–" = nothing to compute from; the most troublesome first. While the samples do not reach back to the period's start, a note says since when it is measured, and the interval the period was measured with (`d.interval`) with a hint that shorter is more precise - for the administrator with a link to Prowlarr's settings, where the measuring in the background is set (`is_admin` of the page: the card's data are the same for everybody). Before the first sample (`d.sampled` false) only the failed queries mean anything: the card shows them with a sentence that availability is measured from now on - how (`d.measuring`, arr/prowlarr/samples.how_measured): continuously by the background loop, once Prowlarr answers it, or with every *Arr sync - without a strip. The strip is one piece per run of days in the same state (OK / failed queries / outage / not measured), as wide as its days (flex-grow); the colours are the theme's (style.css, .day-strip). A screen reader gets how many days of each state (`strip_text`), a mouse the dates of a piece in its title. A long table as the other *Arr tables: the first rows in the card, the whole one in the window.
